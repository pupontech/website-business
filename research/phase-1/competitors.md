## Individual evidence notes

### 1. WebAnts

FACT — Customer and positioning. WebAnts describes its general web business as serving startups, SMEs, and professional-services firms, with a direct designer/developer relationship and no offshoring. Its Astro page covers marketing sites, headless CMS, documentation, portfolios, publications, migrations, and performance audits. Its Ghost page covers custom themes, memberships, newsletters, headless Ghost + Next.js, migrations, setup, performance/SEO, and ongoing support. [1][2][3][6]

FACT — Public prices. Astro marketing sites are listed at £5,000–£12,000 depending on complexity and CMS integration; Starlight documentation sites at £3,000–£8,000; larger content-heavy publications with custom design at £10,000–£25,000. Ghost prices are £1,500–£3,000 for a custom theme on an existing site, £4,000–£10,000 for a full setup with custom theme/membership/newsletter/migration, and from £10,000 for headless Ghost + Next.js. The general website page says custom sites start around £3,000 and scale to £20,000+ for complex builds. [1][2][3]

FACT — Recurring support. The services page advertises website maintenance from £99/mo, with updates, backups, security monitoring, and a four-hour response SLA. The Ghost page describes monthly-retainer or ad-hoc support for version updates, theme tweaks, landing pages, email changes, and content help. [2][4]

FACT — Process and proof. The stated process is a free 30-minute strategy/discovery call, brief and goal analysis, written flat-fee quote within 24 hours, milestone payments, weekly progress, and ownership of deliverables as completed. The portfolio lists 13 projects with industry labels and outcome figures such as traffic, enquiries, bookings, or conversions. [1][3][5]

INTERPRETATION. WebAnts is the broadest direct comparator in this set because its public pages cover both Astro and Ghost alongside general web work and maintenance. Its outcome-oriented portfolio and flat-quote/no-deposit framing are visible market signals; the claimed client outcomes remain vendor-published and were not independently audited here. [3][5][6]

### 2. Motivation Digital

FACT — Customer and positioning. Motivation presents end-to-end Astro strategy, UX, creative, and code for brands that need speed, conversion, and growth. It states that it has been building Astro platforms since 2022 and displays claims of 250+ projects and £276 million in client revenue supported. Those are self-reported claims. [7][8]

FACT — Public prices. Launch is from £15,000 and includes a discovery/strategy workshop, three blueprint templates, five bespoke pages, one lead-acquisition funnel, one conversion funnel with Stripe, CRM/email integration, pre-launch SEO, and an onboarding playbook. Growth is from £24,000 with eight bespoke pages and additional funnels/integrations. Scale is from £49,000 with 12 bespoke pages, multiple audience journeys, custom integrations/APIs, and autonomous-agent configuration. Prices are in GBP and exclude VAT. [7]

FACT — Recurring support and process. The page offers an ongoing retainer for post-launch support, new pages/campaigns, Astro upgrades, edge-configuration improvements, SEO enhancements, and campaign landing pages, with no amount displayed. Six phases are named and described, with defined outputs and client sign-off before completion. [7]

FACT — Portfolio presentation. The reviewed Astro page has an “Astro builds that ship revenue” proof section and links to Trust Center Pro; the about page presents team, logos, and track-record material. A separate work-page extraction was rate-limited during this review, so no additional project claims are recorded. [7][8]

INTERPRETATION. Motivation sells strategic and commercial transformation around Astro, not a low-cost implementation. Its higher public entry points, six-phase process, and fit-check language indicate a premium, selective positioning; this is interpretation from the cited offer, not a claim about private conversion or margin. [7]

### 3. Plinth Studio

FACT — Customer and services. Plinth’s own cost comparison identifies the boutique-studio fit as founders, SaaS companies, and brands that need to convert. Its public tiers include custom design without templates, responsive implementation, SEO, CMS/CRM integration, motion, copywriting, marketing automation, A/B-testing setup, and complex web applications. [9][10]

FACT — Public prices and scope. Launchpad starts at $3,500 for up to seven pages and 2–3 weeks; Accelerator starts at $8,500 for up to 15 pages and 4–5 weeks; Authority starts at $22,000 for up to 30 pages and 8–10 weeks; Enterprise is $50,000+. The page states 50% upfront and 50% on launch, with source files included and an NDA available on request. [9]

FACT — Aftercare. Care is $149/mo and includes hosting oversight, security monitoring, weekly backups, software updates, and one hour of content changes per month. Growth is $499/mo and adds performance monitoring, monthly SEO reporting, three hours of design/development per month, and quarterly conversion review. Partnership is $2,500/mo and adds dedicated retainer hours, quarterly strategy, an A/B-testing roadmap, and priority response SLA. Plans are month-to-month and cancellable. [9]

FACT — Process and evidence gap. The pricing page says to pick a tier and book a call, and publishes revision counts, delivery windows, and package boundaries. No named portfolio or case-study page was captured in the reviewed source set; this is an evidence gap, not a conclusion that none exists. [9][10]

INTERPRETATION. Plinth makes scope, price, ownership, and aftercare unusually legible for a boutique studio. Its care plans are closer to code/site operations than Saintek’s SEO retainers, while its $3,500 entry tier sits above Saintek’s productized landing-page price and below Motivation’s published entry tier. That comparison is descriptive only; it is not a recommendation. [9][11]

### 4. Saintek

FACT — Customer and positioning. Saintek calls itself a small studio for service professionals and lists contractors, attorneys, medical practices, real-estate teams, financial advisors, consultants, and other businesses where a new lead is worth $500+. It frames the offer around fast Astro sites, local search, and AI-readable structured content. [11][12]

FACT — Public prices. Starter Landing is $350 one-time with 3–5-day delivery; Professional Site is $1,950 one-time with 7–10-day delivery; Authority Build is $4,900 one-time with 3–4-week delivery. The WordPress-to-Astro migration add-on is $890 one-time with stated 5–7-day delivery. The page states 50% upfront and 50% on launch; Authority permits three monthly payments. [11]

FACT — Recurring offer. Local SEO is $400/month; Growth SEO is $800/month; AI Visibility is $600/month; Full Authority Stack is $1,500/month. These plans cover GBP, rankings, posts, link outreach, AI visibility, entity/citation work, reporting, and strategy as specified. They are not described as dependency updates, backup, or code-maintenance plans. [11][13]

FACT — Process and deliverables. The about page documents Audit (free 30 minutes), Build (3 days–4 weeks with live staging), Launch (same day, including DNS and listed SEO/analytics setup), and Grow (optional monthly SEO/AEO/GEO). Package pages specify page counts, schema, forms, analytics, revision rounds, and SEO deliverables. [11][12]

INTERPRETATION. Saintek is the clearest productized Astro/local-service comparator: fixed scope, short timelines, public prices, and a lead/search outcome. Its low starting price is not directly comparable to a custom multi-page studio project because the page limits and deliverables differ. No portfolio facts are recorded because the work page could not be re-opened by the extraction service during verification. [11][12]

### 5. Lambda Studio

FACT — Product and target buyer. Lambda Studio sells complete Astro templates. Its catalog covers SaaS/developer tools, portfolios, editorial studios, agencies, design studios, and independent consultants. Lambda Agency specifically includes services, portfolio, blog, contact, legal pages, typed configuration, responsive images, and structured SEO. [14][15]

FACT — Public prices and delivery. The catalog displays Lambda SaaS at $49, Lambda Portfolio at $39, Lambda Minimal at $39, and Lambda Agency at $39. The catalog states that payment/delivery run through Lemon Squeezy, with a download link, source code, typed content files, and README covering setup, content, and deployment. [14]

FACT — Lambda Agency scope and support. The product page lists 11 pages, a live preview, five screenshots, static output, Astro 6.1.8, strict TypeScript, Content Collections + MDX, a case-study layout, versioned releases, and support for installation/build/template bugs. It expressly excludes custom design/development from support. [15]

FACT — License and maintenance ambiguity. The catalog says one purchase covers personal and commercial use across multiple websites, including client projects, while the Lambda Agency page says one purchase covers one website, yours or a client’s, with commercial use. Both statements are retained verbatim in substance; neither is normalized here. The product page reports release/last-update dates of April 27, 2026 and says updates are versioned while the template remains actively maintained. [14][15]

INTERPRETATION. Lambda is not a custom-service agency, but it is a direct substitute for the lowest-complexity agency/studio site and a benchmark for what a buyer may expect from a documented Astro handoff. Its productized price also shows why template competition should be separated from bespoke strategy, content, CMS, and maintenance work. [14][15]

## Cross-competitor observations (not recommendations)

1. FACT: Public pricing appears in several formats: fixed project starts (Motivation, Plinth, Saintek, FrontendWeb), scoped ranges (WebAnts, Naveen’s Ghost guide), hourly retainers (FrontendWeb), monthly care plans (Plinth and Naveen), and subscriptions/templates (Designjoy and Lambda). [1][7][9][11][17][18][21][22]
2. FACT: Several providers make ownership or handoff explicit: WebAnts states ownership of deliverables, Saintek states ownership of design/content/code/domain/analytics on final payment, Plinth includes source files, and Lambda ships source/README documentation. [3][9][12][14][15]
3. FACT: Process transparency is strongest where providers publish defined stages or queues: Motivation’s six phases, Saintek’s four phases, WebAnts’ discovery/quote/milestone model, Plinth’s package boundaries, and Designjoy’s one-request queue. [1][7][9][11][21]
4. INTERPRETATION: Framework language is used as an outcome signal in the Astro set (speed, Core Web Vitals, SEO, ownership, or local search), while Ghost specialists emphasize publishing, themes, memberships/newsletters, migrations, and maintenance. This is a comparative reading of the cited pages, not an independent performance finding. [1][2][7][11][16][19]
5. INTERPRETATION: Price comparisons are unsafe without scope normalization. A $350–$1,950 productized site, a $3,500–$50,000+ boutique engagement, a £15,000+ strategy-led Astro build, and a $39 template solve different portions of the buyer’s problem. [7][9][11][14]
